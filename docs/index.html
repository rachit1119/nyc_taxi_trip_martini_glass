FareSighted: NYC Green Taxi Trip Analysis

Introduction

Transportation systems form the pulse of urban life, shaping how millions of people move through cities every day. In a city as dynamic as New York, taxis play an important role in connecting neighborhoods, supporting mobility, and filling gaps in the public transportation network.

Our project explores the 2023 NYC Green Taxi Trip dataset, which contains detailed records of green taxi trips across New York City. Green taxis, also known as Boro Taxis, primarily operate in the outer boroughs and northern Manhattan.

A central question motivating our analysis is:

How do trip conditions, travel patterns, and congestion-related factors shape the experience of riding an NYC green taxi?

Because the dataset does not contain a direct measure of passenger satisfaction, we use several measurable characteristics as proxies for rider experience, including trip efficiency, pickup and drop-off imbalances, fares, and congestion surcharge.

The project uses interactive visualizations to allow users to investigate these patterns across boroughs, hours of the day, trip distance, trip duration, and congestion conditions.

About the Data

Data Abstraction

The original 2023 NYC Green Taxi Trip dataset contained approximately 787,000 rows and 19 columns. During data exploration, we identified several issues that required cleaning and transformation before the data could be used for analysis.

Two additional datasets were used during the data-wrangling process:

  • Green_Taxi_Trip_Data_Data_Dictionary.xlsx — defines the semantics and categories associated with numeric codes.
  • taxi_zone_lookup.csv — identifies the borough associated with each NYC taxi zone.

The data-wrangling process involved:

  • correcting data formats;
  • mapping numeric category codes to meaningful labels;
  • mapping taxi zones to their corresponding boroughs;
  • creating derived variables for analysis;
  • removing missing and invalid observations; and
  • preparing the dataset for interactive visualization.

The final cleaned dataset contains 727,433 rows and 26 attributes.

Data Preparation

The data was cleaned and transformed before visualization.

Several derived variables were particularly important to the analysis. trip_duration was calculated from the pickup and drop-off timestamps, while trip_dissatisfaction was derived from trip duration and distance.

For the interactive views, the dataset was also aggregated by borough, hour, and trip characteristics where necessary. These transformations allowed the visualizations to remain responsive while preserving the patterns required for analysis.

Because the complete dataset was large, a sample of the cleaned data was used for the exploratory visualizations.

Our Analytical Story

The analysis focuses on three connected aspects of the green taxi experience.

Finding 1 — Trip Efficiency and Satisfaction

How do trip durations and satisfaction vary across boroughs and time blocks?

The first finding investigates whether trips become longer or less efficient during particular times of day and whether these patterns differ across boroughs.

The analysis connects trip duration, trip distance, and trip dissatisfaction to identify trips that take disproportionately long relative to the distance travelled.

Finding 2 — Pickup and Drop-off Imbalances

How do pickup and drop-off patterns reflect supply-demand mismatches and potential wait time expectations, and how is fare related to such imbalance?

The second finding examines differences between pickup and drop-off volumes across boroughs and hours.

Large differences between pickups and drop-offs can provide an indication of potential supply-demand imbalance. The visualization also connects these patterns to average fares.

Finding 3 — Congestion and Surcharge

How does congestion surcharge reflect congestion on routes?

The final finding investigates the relationship between trip volume and congestion surcharge throughout the day.

By comparing hourly trip volumes with average congestion surcharge, we can examine whether periods of greater taxi activity coincide with higher congestion-related charges.

General Takeaways

The dashboards allow users to investigate:

  • how trip efficiency varies across boroughs and time blocks;
  • where pickup and drop-off imbalances occur;
  • how fares change alongside pickup and drop-off patterns;
  • how congestion surcharge varies throughout the day;
  • whether trip volume and congestion surcharge are related; and
  • how these factors can be used as proxies for the rider experience.

The goal is not to claim that these measures directly represent passenger satisfaction. Instead, they provide observable indicators that allow us to investigate aspects of efficiency, availability, pricing, and congestion.
